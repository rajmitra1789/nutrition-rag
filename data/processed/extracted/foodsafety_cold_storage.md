# Cold Food Storage Charts

Follow the guidelines below for storing food in the refrigerator and freezer. The short time limits for home-refrigerated foods will help keep them from spoiling or becoming dangerous to eat. The guidelines for freezer storage are for quality only—frozen foods stored continuously at 0°F (-18°C) or below can be kept indefinitely.

Refrigerator temperatures in this chart are 40°F (4°C) or below. Freezer temperatures are 0°F (-18°C) or below.

## Salad

| Food | Type | Refrigerator [40°F (4°C) or below] | Freezer [0°F (-18°C) or below] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Salad | Egg, chicken, ham, tuna, and macaroni salads | 3 to 4 days | Does not freeze well |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hot dogs | Opened package | 1 week | 1 to 2 months |
| Hot dogs | Unopened package | 2 weeks | 1 to 2 months |

## Bacon and sausage

| Food | Type | Refrigerator [40°F (4°C) or below] | Freezer [0°F (-18°C) or below] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bacon and sausage | Bacon | 1 week | 1 month |

| Bacon and sausage | Sausage, fully cooked, from chicken, turkey, pork, or beef | 1 week | 1 to 2 months |
| Bacon and sausage | Sausage, purchased frozen | After cooking, 3-4 days | 1-2 months from date of purchase |

## Ham

| Food | Type | Refrigerator [40°F (4°C) or below] | Freezer [0°F (-18°C) or below] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ham | Fresh, uncured, uncooked | 3 to 5 days | 6 months |
| Ham | Fresh, uncured, cooked | 3 to 4 days | 3 to 4 months |
| Ham | Cured, cook-before-eating, uncooked | 5 to 7 days or “use by” date | 3 to 4 months |
| Ham | Fully-cooked, vacuum-sealed at plant, unopened | 2 weeks or “use by” date | 1 to 2 months |
| Ham | Cooked, store-wrapped, whole | 1 week | 1 to 2 months |
| Ham | Cooked, store-wrapped, slices, half, or spiral cut | 3 to 5 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Ham | Country ham, cooked | 1 week | 1 month |
| Ham | Canned, labeled "Keep Refrigerated," unopened | 6 to 9 months | Do not freeze |
| Ham | Canned, shelf-stable, opened Note: An unopened, shelf-stable, canned ham can be stored at room temperature for 2 years. | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Ham | Prosciutto, Parma or Serrano ham, dry Italian or Spanish type, cut | 2 to 3 months | 1 month |

## Eggs

| Food | Type | Refrigerator [40°F (4°C) or below] | Freezer [0°F (-18°C) or below] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eggs | Raw eggs in shell | 3 to 5 weeks | Do not freeze in shell. Beat yolks and whites together, then freeze. |
| Eggs | Raw egg whites and yolks Note: Yolks do not freeze well | 2 to 4 days | 12 months |
| Eggs | Raw egg accidentally frozen in shell Note: Toss any frozen eggs with a broken shell | Use immediately after thawing | Keep frozen, then refrigerate to thaw |
| Eggs | Hard-cooked eggs | 1 week | Do not freeze |
| Eggs | Egg substitutes, liquid, unopened | 1 week | Do not freeze |
| Eggs | Egg substitutes, liquid, opened | 3 days | Do not freeze |
| Eggs | Egg substitutes, frozen, unopened | After thawing, 1 week or refer to “use by” date | 12 months |
| Eggs | Egg substitutes, frozen, opened | After thawing, 3 to 4 days or refer to “use by” date | Do not freeze |
| Eggs | Casseroles with eggs | After baking, 3 to 4 days | After baking, 2 to 3 months |
| Eggs | Eggnog, commercial | 3 to 5 days | 6 months |
| Eggs | Eggnog, homemade | 2 to 4 days | Do not freeze |
| Eggs | Pies: Pumpkin or pecan | After baking, 3 to 4 days | After baking, 1 to 2 months |
| Eggs | Pies: Custard and chiffon | After baking, 3 to 4 days | Do not freeze |
| Eggs | Quiche with filling | After baking, 3 to 5 days | After baking, 2 to 3 months |
